# Curiosities

## Options that were never finished

- **Gwent AI difficulty and aggression.** The player keeps two Gwent settings, an AI difficulty
  (Easy by default) and an AI aggression (Defensive by default), with everything needed to read
  and change them, but nothing in the game ever uses them. A choice of difficulty and play style
  for the Gwent AI was planned and left unfinished; Better Gwent's levels and NPC deck option cover
  that ground today.
- **A difficulty and a play style for each opponent.** The quests that start a Gwent match mark
  each opponent with a difficulty and an aggression. Fifteen matches are marked Hard (among them
  Thaler, Lambert, Dijkstra, Lugos the Mad, the Card Prodigy and the four decks of the Passiflora
  tournament) and three with the most aggressive style, called "all I have". The game never hands
  these marks to the AI, so every opponent plays the same way. The game itself knows five
  aggression levels (Defensive, Normal, Aggressive, Very Aggressive, All I Have) and three
  difficulties (Easy, Medium, Hard).

## Cut and unused pieces of the Gwent screens

- **A card type that plays on the opponent's row,** like a Horn placed on the enemy's side: the
  rules for placing it are written, but no card uses it.
- **A sound for agile cards** that nothing plays.
- **A first version of the Gwent tutorial** (16 entries: the discard pile, faction abilities,
  inspecting a card, skipping the tutorial, one about the Crinfrid Reavers) that nothing uses, and
  a ready glossary page about leader cards that nothing unlocks.
- No animation or icon inside the Gwent screens is left without use.

## Developer tools left in the game

The game keeps developer console commands for Gwent: start a quick match against any NPC deck,
skip the tutorials, force the player's faction, receive every card of a faction, win or lose a
match at once, and watch a match **played by the AI on both sides**. They are only reachable with
the developer console enabled. The AI-against-AI match is a promising way to check Better Gwent in
the real game without a person playing.

## Notes left by the developers

The card and item files keep the developers' own notes, for example:

- every Arachas once also called the **Arachas Behemoth**; in the final game only the Behemoth
  calls the Arachas;
- **Skellige Storm** was in every deck, with a note to change its icon if it ever became a
  Skellige-only card;
- the collection achievement was changed to count **unique** cards only;
- Philippa Eilhart was marked as not yet in any NPC deck, and some cards as rewards to be placed.

## Small facts

- A new game gives Yarpen Zigrin and a Redanian Foot Soldier on top of the starting deck.
- If a quest asks for an NPC deck that does not exist, the game quietly uses an easy Nilfgaard deck.
- Two of the game's own Skellige NPC decks (easy and normal) hold Avallac'h twice, although heroes
  are meant to be unique. Better Gwent's NPC decks never repeat a hero.
- The game's own NPC decks often hold two copies of a named character that is not a hero
  (Vattier de Rideaux, Villentretenmerth, Sweers, Botchlings and others), while a player can own
  only one. Better Gwent keeps this standard of the game.
- Of the 56 NPC deck lists in the game, all but one are used by quests, the game's scripts or the
  tutorial; the unused one is the Northern Realms tournament deck of Toussaint (see
  [Bugs of the base game](base-game-bugs.md)).
